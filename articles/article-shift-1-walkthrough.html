Shift 1 Walkthrough: Complete First Day Survival Guide (2026)

Everything you need to survive your first shift — room navigation, anomaly patterns, item priorities, coin optimization, and the common mistakes that kill new players.

Shift Walkthrough
July 27, 2026 ~14 min read

Shift 1 in Animal Hospital Anomaly is where every player's journey begins — and where most players make the mistakes that haunt them later. This complete walkthrough covers everything from the moment you spawn in the lobby to the final coin count: room-by-room navigation, anomaly detection patterns specific to Shift 1, optimal item loadouts, coin-saving strategies, and the critical errors that cause sanity failure on what should be the easiest shift.

Whether you're a brand-new player or someone repeating Shift 1 to bank coins before pushing deeper, this guide will help you run a flawless first day. Master these fundamentals and Shift 2 becomes dramatically easier. Skip them and you'll be rebuilding your coin reserve every third run.

Shift 1 Overview: What You're Walking Into

Shift 1 is the game's tutorial shift, but don't let that fool you into complacency. The difficulty is intentionally low — fewer patients, simpler anomalies, longer timers — but the systems are identical to later shifts. Every habit you build here compounds. Every shortcut you take here becomes a liability in Shift 4.

  • Patient count: 5 to 7 patients per shift (randomized)
  • Shift duration: Approximately 8-12 minutes depending on patient flow
  • Anomaly frequency: Low — roughly 1 in 3 patients has an anomaly
  • Anomaly types: Primarily visual anomalies; no CCTV or photo anomalies yet
  • Emergency events: None — emergencies begin at Shift 3
  • Coin reward: 50-100 coins per patient treated + 100 coin completion bonus
  • Recommended class: Intern (free) or Nurse (500 coins)

The key insight most beginners miss: Shift 1 is not about speed — it's about building systems. The player who treats 4 patients with perfect anomaly detection will out-earn the player who treats 7 patients but misses two anomalies. Sanity loss from missed anomalies costs more than the coins you earn from rushing.

Pre-Shift Setup: Loadout and Preparation

Before clicking "Start Shift," make three decisions that determine your run quality. The first two cost nothing; the third costs coins but pays for itself within two shifts.

1. Class Selection

Intern is the free starting class. You get 3 inventory slots, standard sanity drain, and no bonuses. For your first 3-5 Shift 1 runs, Intern is intentional — you're building fundamentals without class bonuses masking your weaknesses. Once you can consistently identify visual anomalies by sight, upgrade to Nurse (500 coins) for the +3 inventory slots. The Nurse's extra slots let you carry a sanity restoration item, a treatment item, and still have room for emergency pickup.

2. Starting Inventory

At the Pharmacy counter, grab these three items before the first patient arrives:

  • Splint — the most common Shift 1 condition (Broken Leg). Having one ready saves a Pharmacy trip.
  • Ice Pack — the second most common condition (Fever). Stack one in your inventory.
  • Chocolate — +20 sanity restoration. Your sanity insurance policy. If your sanity drops below 50%, eat it immediately.

3. Shop Upgrade Priority

If you have 500 coins saved, buy the DNA Analysis upgrade from the shop. This reveals an anomaly's "true form" in the paperwork layer, making detection nearly foolproof. Without DNA Analysis, you must rely on visual and CCTV checks alone — which is fine for Shift 1, but the upgrade pays dividends through Shift 3 and beyond.

Room Navigation: The Shift 1 Layout

The hospital in Shift 1 has a simple linear layout. Memorizing the room order saves precious seconds per patient cycle and reduces the cognitive load that causes anomaly-check misses.

Shift 1 Hospital Layout — Room Order and Function
Room Function Key Notes Anomaly Check Layer
Lobby Check-in / Discharge Starting point; all patients enter here Paperwork only
Pharmacy Item pickup Grab treatment items and sanity restoratives None
Room 1 Patient treatment Closest to pharmacy; fastest turnaround Visual + CCTV
Room 2 Patient treatment Middle distance; moderate walk time Visual + CCTV
Room 3 Patient treatment Farthest room; longest walk Visual + CCTV
CCTV Station Monitor rooms Switch between Room 1/2/3 feeds CCTV anomaly check

Pro tip: Treat patients in Room 1 first, then Room 2, then Room 3. This minimizes total walking distance. On Shift 1 with only 5-7 patients, this routing saves 30-45 seconds across the entire shift — time you can use for more thorough anomaly checks.

The 6-Step Shift 1 Patient Workflow

Every patient follows the same workflow. Run it identically for every single patient until it becomes muscle memory. Variation is the enemy of consistency.

  1. Check in at Lobby. Click the patient in the waiting area. They'll be assigned a room number. Note the room — don't rely on memory when you're learning.
  2. Go to the assigned room. Read the diagnosis chart on the wall. It lists the patient's condition (e.g., "Broken Leg," "Fever," "Headache").
  3. Go to Pharmacy. Grab the matching treatment item. Splint for Broken Leg, Ice Pack for Fever, Painkillers for Headache, Bandage for Bleeding.
  4. Return to patient and use item. Click the patient with the item in your inventory. A treatment animation plays. Watch for the green checkmark.
  5. Check anomaly detection layers. This is the critical step. In Shift 1, check: Visual (does the patient look wrong? Extra eyes? Wrong skin color?) and CCTV (go to CCTV Station, switch to the patient's room, look for visual distortions). If both layers pass, the patient is clear.
  6. Discharge at Lobby. Return to the lobby and click the patient to discharge them. You receive coins immediately.
Advanced Workflow Tip: On Shift 1, batch your Pharmacy trips. If Room 1 has a Broken Leg patient AND Room 2 has a Broken Leg patient, grab two Splints in one trip. This saves a round-trip walk and cuts your total shift time by 15-20 seconds. Never batch more than two items — you'll forget which item goes to which patient, and confusion causes anomaly-check skips.

Shift 1 Anomaly Patterns: What to Look For

Shift 1 only spawns visual anomalies — no CCTV-only or paperwork-only anomalies yet. This is intentional: the game teaches you the visual detection layer before adding complexity. Here are the visual anomaly patterns you'll encounter in Shift 1:

Shift 1 Visual Anomaly Detection Quick Reference
Anomaly Name Visual Sign Location on Patient Severity
Extra Eyes Third eye visible on forehead or cheek Face/Head High — always reject
Hollow Face Faceless — smooth skin where features should be Face/Head High — always reject
Skinwalker Skin texture shifts/moves independently Arms, neck, or torso Critical — immediate sanity drain
Bed Monster Patient's shadow moves independently Around the bed Medium — reject if shadow moves
Wrong Uniform Staff uniform is wrong color or has extra elements Clothing Low — easy to miss, but reject
Floating Limbs Arm or leg slightly detached from body Extremities Medium — reject

Critical rule: If you see any visual anomaly sign, do not discharge the patient. Instead, click the "Reject" button on the patient's chart. Rejecting costs you the coin reward for that patient but preserves your sanity. Discharging an anomalous patient costs 10-15 sanity points — far more valuable than 50-100 coins.

Common Shift 1 Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

These are the errors that kill Shift 1 runs for new players. Every single one is avoidable with the right habit:

Mistake 1: Skipping Anomaly Checks on "Easy" Patients

The patient with a simple Broken Leg condition who looks perfectly normal still has a chance of being anomalous. In Shift 1, roughly 1 in 3 patients has an anomaly. If you skip the visual check because "this one looks fine," you're gambling with your sanity. Always check. Every patient. No exceptions.

Mistake 2: Carrying Too Many Items

New players tend to stockpile items "just in case." With only 3 inventory slots (Intern), carrying 2 Splints and an Ice Pack means no room for sanity restoration. If your sanity drops and you have no Chocolate, you must walk back to the Pharmacy — wasting 20-30 seconds. Keep your inventory lean: 1 treatment item, 1 sanity item, 1 flex slot.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Sanity Below 50%

Sanity drains from three sources: treating anomalous patients (10-15 points), proximity to anomalous patients (2-5 points per second in same room), and passive drain over time (1 point per 30 seconds). If your sanity drops to 0%, the shift ends in failure. Use sanity restoration items at 50%, not 20%. At 20%, one unexpected anomaly hit ends your run.

Mistake 4: Rushing the Room 3 Patient

Room 3 is the farthest from the Pharmacy and Lobby. New players rush this patient because they want to "finish the shift." Rushing causes missed anomaly checks. Slow down. The extra 15 seconds you spend checking Room 3 is cheaper than a sanity failure.

Mistake 5: Not Counting Patients

The game doesn't display a "remaining patients" counter on Shift 1. If you lose count, you might prematurely end the shift (missing coin income) or keep searching for a patient who doesn't exist (wasting time). After each discharge, mentally note: "That was patient 4 of 6." The lobby waiting area shows remaining patients — glance at it after every discharge.

Coin Optimization: Maximizing Shift 1 Income

Shift 1 is your primary coin source in the first 3-5 hours of gameplay. Treat it as a farming run, not a challenge run. Here's how to maximize income:

The Shift 1 Coin Math

  • 5-7 patients per shift × 50-100 coins each = 250-700 coins
  • Completion bonus: 100 coins
  • Total per shift: 350-800 coins
  • Average shift time: 10 minutes
  • Coins per minute: 35-80

This is competitive with Shift 2 coin rates but with dramatically lower difficulty. If you're struggling with Shift 2, it's more efficient to repeat Shift 1 and bank coins than to fail Shift 2 repeatedly.

Coin-Saving Habits

  • Never buy items you don't need. If you already have a Splint in inventory, don't grab another one "just in case."
  • Use free sanity restoration first. The couch in the break room restores +5 sanity for free. Use it before consuming Chocolate.
  • Don't reject non-anomalous patients. Rejecting costs the coin reward. Only reject when you're certain the patient is anomalous.
  • Complete every shift. Quitting early forfeits the 100-coin completion bonus. Even if your sanity is low, push through to the end — death is the only penalty for 0% sanity, and you keep all coins earned before death.

When to End Shift 1 and Move to Shift 2

Shift 1 is a safe farming ground, but staying too long delays your progression. Move to Shift 2 when you meet these criteria:

  • You can treat 5+ patients without referencing a condition-to-item chart
  • You identify visual anomalies by sight 80%+ of the time
  • You consistently finish with above 50% sanity
  • You have at least 500 coins saved (for the Nurse class)
  • You've completed Shift 1 at least 3 times

If you meet four of those five criteria, push to Shift 2. If not, run Shift 1 again. There is no penalty for repeating — the coins add up and the muscle memory compounds.

New Player Tip: The single biggest predictor of Shift 1 success is checking every patient for anomalies before discharging. It's not item knowledge, it's not room navigation, it's not class selection. It's the discipline to run the full anomaly check even when you're tired, even when the patient looks normal, especially when you're rushing. Build that habit on Shift 1 and it carries you through all 5 shifts.

Shift 1 Checklist: Pre-Flight Verification

Use this checklist before every Shift 1 run until the habits become automatic:

  • ☐ Class selected (Intern for learning, Nurse for farming)
  • ☐ Inventory: 1 Splint, 1 Ice Pack, 1 Chocolate
  • ☐ Sanity at 100% (use couch in break room if not)
  • ☐ Room order memorized: Room 1 → Room 2 → Room 3
  • ☐ Anomaly visual signs reviewed (Extra Eyes, Hollow Face, Skinwalker)
  • ☐ Treatment items identified: Splint/Broken Leg, Ice Pack/Fever, Painkillers/Headache, Bandage/Bleeding

Run this checklist in your head before clicking "Start Shift." Thirty seconds of preparation prevents thirty minutes of coin loss from a sanity failure.

Advanced: Shift 1 Speed Techniques

Once you've mastered the basics, these techniques squeeze extra efficiency out of every Shift 1 run:

1. Pre-Positioning

Stand at the Pharmacy counter between patients. When a new patient spawns, you're one click away from grabbing their treatment item. This saves 5-10 seconds per patient cycle.

2. Visual Anomaly Pre-Check

Before reading the diagnosis chart, glance at the patient's face and body. If you see Extra Eyes or Hollow Face, you already know they're anomalous — no need to grab a treatment item first. Reject immediately and save the Pharmacy trip.

3. Lobby Batching

If two patients are waiting in the lobby, check both in before treating either. This lets you route-optimize: if both are assigned to Room 1, treat them back-to-back. If one is Room 1 and one is Room 3, treat Room 1 first (closer) while the Room 3 patient waits — their timer doesn't start until you check them in.

4. Sanity Threshold Discipline

Advanced players use a 60% sanity threshold instead of 50%. At 60%, eat Chocolate. This 10% buffer accounts for the sanity drain from proximity to anomalous patients — you'll take 2-5 points per second while standing next to one, and the buffer prevents accidental 0% deaths.
